The website challenge

Explain technical services without making the customer decode them.

Bannister Communications serves homes, shops, industrial sites and rural properties across several regional Queensland communities. The website needed to explain connected areas including CCTV, alarms, active deterrence, access and network cabling while keeping the offer understandable to non-technical visitors.

Security services also require a high level of credibility. The live website supports the service information with licence details, experience, product context, genuine Google reviews and clear local coverage.

Key content inputs

  • Security licence and insurance context
  • CCTV and alarm system categories
  • Residential, commercial and rural property types
  • Authorised equipment relationships
  • Local coverage and support
  • Google review evidence

Information architecture

Connect services, equipment and coverage without overwhelming the menu.

The main navigation keeps Home, About, Services, Blog and Contact at the top level. Service and equipment sections then give visitors more specific paths. Dedicated coverage pages support genuinely different areas rather than repeating a page for every nearby suburb.

  1. Home
  2. About
  3. Services
  4. CCTV and Alarm Details
  5. Regional Coverage Pages
  6. Equipment Context
  7. Blog
  8. Contact

The homepage introduces system types and practical use cases before showing equipment. This order helps the customer understand the solution first, with brands and features in a supporting role.

Trust and contact

Put verifiable credentials near the customer journey.

The site displays the security licence, local service area, experience and authorised product relationships near the core offer. Reviews appear as attributed customer feedback. This gives visitors several kinds of evidence without relying on unsupported superlatives.

Quote and call actions are available from the main content and mobile action area. WhatsApp provides another route for customers who prefer messaging. The contact options remain connected to service and coverage context rather than appearing only on a final contact page.
